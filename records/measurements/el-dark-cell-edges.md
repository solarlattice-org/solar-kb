name: Dark cell edges in EL
aliases:
- darkening of cell edge regions
---
In an electroluminescence image, the borders of the cells look darker, while the cell centers stay brighter. To take the image, a DC current is fed through the module and a camera records the light it gives off. This is done in a dark environment, usually at night, because daylight drowns out the signal. With metallization corrosion, the dark cell borders show moisture moving in through the module's rear side and the gaps between cells. The front side of each cell then corrodes, starting at its edges. With potential induced degradation, EL shows dark cells at the module boundaries, with the cell edges darkest near the frame. This is commonly blamed on current flowing through a lowered parallel resistance instead of the PN junction. Night-time field EL of modules with PID has been taken with a consumer DSLR camera, using an accumulated exposure of around four minutes. In PID-tested heterojunction glass/glass mini-modules, the damage started at the cell edges and spread inward over time. This was linked to moisture ingress helping sodium move through the module. Corrosion often appears together with delamination and yellowed encapsulant, so check the module visually as well.
