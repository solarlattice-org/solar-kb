Insulation resistance testing measures the resistance between the live parts of a PV array, its cables or a module and earth or the module frame. It is the only reliable way to confirm an insulation failure, because poor insulation often cannot be seen. Before testing, isolate the array from the inverter, keep unauthorised people away and disconnect surge protection devices or other equipment that could affect the reading or be damaged. Then connect the tester between earth and the array cables or combiner bus bar. The test voltage and the minimum acceptable resistance depend on the system voltage and are listed in the standard tables. Readings are taken in megaohms, and each circuit must meet its minimum value. Use this method to confirm cable defects, connector defects, glass breakage and junction box failure. Each of these faults can cause an insulation fault that may make the inverter trip or shut down the plant. If a dry test gives doubtful results, or you suspect installation or manufacturing defects, a wet insulation test can help find the fault. It simulates rain or dew and is good at exposing damaged wiring, poorly closed or cracked junction boxes, backsheet punctures and indoor-rated connectors. Faults may only show up when the array is humid, for example after rain or in early-morning dew. A dry test alone can therefore miss them. PV circuits stay live in daylight, so there is a shock hazard during the test. Wear protective equipment, make sure nobody touches metal surfaces, module backs or terminals, use a tester that discharges itself automatically, and confirm the system is de-energised before removing the leads.
