Component aging is the slow change in a material, component or system's properties over its operating time, driven by the stresses it is exposed to. In PV equipment, aging usually shows up as lower performance, meaning lost power. Climate shapes how equipment ages because different materials react differently to temperature, humidity, UV light, rain and wind. Combinations of these factors also create secondary loads such as snow load and dust soiling. Inside a module, aging affects individual parts. For example, aged cell interconnect ribbons gain resistance, and parts such as bypass diodes, ribbons and cells already differ in quality when new. If a connector, cable, junction box, backsheet or glass fails, the whole module can lose its power or become a safety fault, and the loss can reach the whole string. Other parts, such as bypass diodes, can fail partly without a total loss. Polymer materials are especially sensitive to aging. Under heat, post-crystallization can make them shrink, warp and crack. Climate-specific accelerated aging tests can recreate field effects such as cell cracks and broken cell connectors. Their results help when choosing equipment for a given climate and when planning operation and maintenance for that climate.
