Self-discharge is the loss of charge that happens while a battery sits in storage or is not used. Higher ambient temperature makes self-discharge much stronger. Calendar aging also matters: an unused battery loses usable capacity over time. Too little charging, deep discharge and temperature add to this aging. A lithium-ion tool battery that is not used can run itself flat in about six months, so charge it periodically. Some PV battery products state a maximum storage time of six months. If a home battery is left switched on with all its functions disabled, it will probably drain to zero state of energy and need service from the manufacturer. If the battery temperature sensor is missing or faulty, the inverter can deep-discharge the battery over time, which can destroy it. Some inverters disconnect themselves as a precaution to prevent deep discharge. If a battery reaches a critically low state of energy, switch it to a backup-only mode so it stays charged while the site problem is fixed.
