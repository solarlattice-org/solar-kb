Salt mist is airborne salt, typical of coastal sites. It gets into PV modules and speeds up corrosion and potential induced degradation. Modules in coastal areas can degrade heavily from salt-driven corrosion, and faster PID has been reported when modules were sprayed with salt mist. Salt also corrodes mounting structures, and more salt means more corrosion. At salt-surface desert sites such as the Atacama, night-time condensation combined with salt left structures rusted after about a year in service. Corrosive atmospheres can damage seals, reduce insulation effectiveness and cause arcing that may lead to fire. Inverter manuals treat the area within 500 m of the coast as salt-affected. They say not to install inverters outdoors there, or to consult the manufacturer first. How much salt fog settles depends on sea wind, precipitation, humidity, terrain and forest cover. One manufacturer allows inverters and optimizers to be installed at least 50 m from an ocean or other saline shoreline, provided salt water does not splash directly on the equipment. Choose equipment rated for salt exposure: an inverter with an IEC 61701 corrosivity class C5 rating for use at least 0.5 km from the coast, electronics rated to IEC 60068-2-11 salt mist, and modules tested with the IEC 61701 salt mist corrosion test. Keep battery modules away from salt. For structures, pick suitable materials and an appropriate surface finish.
