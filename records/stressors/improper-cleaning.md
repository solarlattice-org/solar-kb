id: stressor:improper-cleaning
type: Stressor
name: Improper cleaning
aliases:
- inappropriate cleaning techniques
---
Improper cleaning means using unsuitable methods, such as mechanical removal or the wrong cleaning agents, which damage or strip the coatings on the module front glass. It is one cause of glass corrosion or abrasion, which reduces or removes the benefit of anti-reflective and anti-soiling coatings. In dry cleaning, small dust particles can scratch the glass and weaken the coating's optical properties, and the mechanical force of cleaning can damage the cells inside the module. In cleaning tests, reflectance changed a lot on several coated module types, which shows the anti-reflective coating was removed; nylon brushes and fine sand were more abrasive, and uncoated glass showed little or no effect. The risk is highest at desert plants, where frequent dry cleaning is needed and repeated cleaning wears the glass surface, reducing light transmission and module output. Active cleaning can wear down anti-reflective coatings and cause a small drop in efficiency, even though the immediate gain from cleaning is large. To reduce exposure, follow the module manufacturer's cleaning recommendations, use demineralized water with a mild approved detergent, stay below the maximum water pressure the manufacturer specifies (going over it can void the warranty), and use water close to the module temperature to avoid thermal shock. Before use, qualify a cleaning process with an accelerated test that uses the planned cleaning device, module type and expected soiling, and do not let anti-soiling coatings be damaged by unsuitable cleaning processes.
