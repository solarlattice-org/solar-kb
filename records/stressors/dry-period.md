Extended dry periods are stretches without meaningful rain, during which dust settles on module glass and is not washed away. The dust cuts glass transmittance and lowers array current, so less light reaches the cells while the plant stays online. This drives granular dust soiling losses. In the field this shows up as a gradual drop in irradiance-normalized production through the dry period, often followed by partial or full recovery after real rain or washing. Nearby roads, exposed soil, farming, construction, prevailing wind and row edges can make the loss uneven across the site. To diagnose it, look for dry periods where output falls without availability, clipping, curtailment or irradiance sensor problems. Then compare against soiling stations, rainfall, cleaning logs and reference blocks. Exposure is reduced by cleaning: in wet tropical climates weekly cleaning is recommended during dry spells, adjusted to how fast dust builds up. Cleaning triggers should be set by the value of the lost energy, using a soiling model calibrated with operational data.
