P50 and P90 are confidence levels attached to a predicted annual energy yield. P90 is the yield that should be exceeded in 90 percent of cases and P50 is the yield exceeded in 50 percent of cases, which makes P50 the expected value. Other levels such as P75 are used too, and a good yield report for investors gives at least the P50 and P90 values. The gap between P50 and P90 comes from uncertainty in the solar resource data, uncertainty in the modelling process and year-to-year variation in the solar resource. Resource data uncertainty usually has more effect than modelling accuracy, because energy yield depends roughly linearly on plane-of-array irradiance. Lenders often use a one-year P90 to cover the yearly debt payments and a ten-year P90 to cover resource variation across a decade. The exact requirement depends on how the project is financed. Combined uncertainty becomes smaller over the life of the plant. On an uncertainty chart, P90 is the lower bound and P10 is the upper bound. During operation, O&M reports often compare actual irradiance, production and revenue with the expected P50, P75 and P90 values each month, quarter or year.
