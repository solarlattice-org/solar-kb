Liquid-immersed transformers have their core and coils sitting in insulating oil, which insulates them and carries heat away. Tanks and radiators can start leaking oil, most often at connections, headers and individual pipes. Bushings can also leak through a gasket, a soldered or welded seal, or a cracked insulator, and a falling oil level is a warning sign. Spilled oil that reaches land or waterways puts human health, drinking water and natural resources at risk. Oil leaks are serious maintenance and environmental problems, so treat them with care and get them repaired instead of ignoring them. Regular infrared scans and physical inspection of tanks, radiators, pump piping and heat exchangers help find leaks early. In the United States, the EPA Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure rule (40 CFR Part 112) aims to stop facilities from discharging oil into navigable waters and their shorelines.
