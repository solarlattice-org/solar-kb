Heat stress happens when workers do physical work in hot conditions. It can cause heat illness, and in severe cases heat stroke, which can kill. Heavy work raises the risk. NIOSH examples of heavy work include climbing, carrying equipment or supplies weighing 40 pounds or more, and using hand tools for long periods. Even moderate work at 108 °F carries a high risk of heat injury and calls for extreme caution. A work/rest schedule is the main control. For example, NIOSH says heavy work at 104 °F should follow 20 minutes of work with 40 minutes of rest. Drink water often and have coworkers watch each other for signs of heat illness. In the NIOSH case study, crews who did this got affected workers to medical care early, and those workers recovered. In extreme heat, a work/rest schedule alone may not remove the risk. Use all of these controls together.
