Regular maintenance means planned visual and physical inspections and checks that key plant components still work properly. It is done at set intervals that follow the equipment maker's manuals and the O&M manuals. For connector and cable defects, replace any component that is a direct safety risk. Then inspect regularly to track the condition of the parts that were left in place. Regular system inspections are also recommended to keep watch on backsheet cracking and backsheet chalking. For chalking, they help track how the observed failure progresses. Typical tasks include visual inspection and thermography of inverter wiring boxes, keeping cooling systems free of debris, and checking cable entries once a year, resealing them when needed. One inverter maker recommends servicing once a year and adjusting the interval to site conditions. More frequent visits in the first year may be needed to find the right interval. Preventive maintenance aims to maximize output, prevent more costly failures and extend system life. Inverter damage caused by skipped maintenance may not be covered by the warranty. The main limit is cost: the amount of scheduled maintenance has to be weighed against yield and cash flow. If the work needs downtime, doing it at night is considered best practice.
