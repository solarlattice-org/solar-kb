id: mit:mechanical-stress-reduction
type: Mitigation
name: Mechanical stress reduction
---
Mechanical stress reduction means cutting down the physical loads on modules that make cell interconnection failure get worse over time. It goes together with replacing modules whose interconnect failures are already electrically active. Reducing stress slows further damage but does not repair broken connections. Interconnections are stressed mechanically during transport, and in operation by repeated wind load, hail and thermomechanical cycling. Static or cyclic mechanical loading can cause broken interconnects, solder bond failures, cell breakage and glass breakage. The joint between the busbars and the string interconnect ribbon fatigues more easily than the other interconnections, so it needs the most protection. When wind or hail bends a framed module, the rear side is put under tension. Microcracks left from transport or installation can then grow. A loose or detached junction box also puts mechanical stress on its internal contacts and can break them, so fix junction box attachment as part of this action. Hotspots from partial shading add thermomechanical stress on the interconnections, so dealing with mechanical loads alone may not stop the fatigue.
