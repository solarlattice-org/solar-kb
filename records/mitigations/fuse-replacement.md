DC fuse replacement means swapping out a damaged DC string fuse on the inverter or combiner input. Sungrow lists it as a corrective step for its PV abnormal and PVx abnormal alarms. Sungrow puts the fuse check after other checks: first look for shading or dirt on the modules and for loose or missing string connections, then check the fuse for that string. If none of these causes is found and the alarm stays on, contact the manufacturer. For an arc fault alarm, disconnect the DC side and look for loose fuses, loose terminals or poor contacts. Tighten anything loose and replace any burnt parts before reconnecting. Only a qualified person should replace string fuses. Before starting, turn off the DC disconnect, the AC disconnect and the AC branch breaker. Use a DC clamp meter to confirm that no current flows through the fuse, and cover the array with dark opaque sheets. To replace the fuse, hold only the plastic tab of the fuse extractor and pull it straight up. Load the new fuse into the extractor, line the fuse caps up with the clips and push down until the fuse snaps into place. Size each new fuse for its string. CPS says the fuse must be rated for at least 1000VDC, and the ampere rating is generally about 1.56 times the module Isc. On the CPS units, 25A or 30A fuses must not sit in adjacent holders. Fuses from other makers, or fuses of the wrong size, can damage the equipment or make it unsafe to work on. CPS warranty does not cover damage caused by incompatible fuses.
