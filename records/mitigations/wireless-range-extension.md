Wireless range extension improves a weak radio link between the inverter and the Wi-Fi router or access point, so the inverter can find and stay connected to the network. Use it when the inverter cannot see the wireless network, sees it but cannot connect, or keeps showing connection-loss messages, after you have checked that the router is on, in range and working normally. The simplest step is to move the router as close to the inverter as you can, keeping in mind the materials the signal must pass through, and to keep the tip of the antenna, which is a dead zone, from pointing at the router. You can also install a Wi-Fi repeater partway between the inverter and the router, placed so that it avoids the worst obstacles. On some SMA inverters, an Antenna Extension Kit accessory can be fitted inside the inverter, including as a retrofit, to extend its radio range. Check the signal quality during installation, where it is shown in dBm. Even a good radio link can drop out from time to time, so SMA generally recommends a wired Ethernet connection instead.
