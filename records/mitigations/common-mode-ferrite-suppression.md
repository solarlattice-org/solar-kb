Common-mode ferrite suppression means fitting ferrite cores or common mode chokes to conductors. It is used when a PV installation is suspected of causing radio frequency interference. Common mode current flowing on antenna feedlines or other conductors is one of the most frequent causes of interference complaints. Chokes can be fitted on coaxial antenna feedlines. Ferrite cores can be clipped onto the AC cables, the DC strings and the communication cables. Other measures are often used at the same time: an extra mains EMC filter, a check of antenna baluns, grounding and bonding, and cable routing that keeps RF and power circuits apart. Running the radio equipment from batteries shows whether the interference is getting in through the radio's power supply. If ferrites and filters bring no measurable improvement, further investigation is needed before any conclusion is drawn about the source. Some inverter spare-part kits include ferrite parts as separate listed items, so refit them when you replace the matching assembly.
