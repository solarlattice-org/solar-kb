ESD-safe handling protects inverter circuit boards and other electronic parts from electrostatic discharge, which can damage them beyond repair or destroy the inverter. It applies to any work inside an open device where electronic components, terminals or assemblies could be touched, such as board swaps and retrofitting extra circuit boards. Before you touch any component, ground yourself by touching a grounded enclosure part or another grounded object, and wear an ESD wrist strap the whole time you work on assemblies. Do not touch the PCB unless you need to. Wear suitable protective gear, such as cotton clothing and ESD-rated gloves, and follow the ESD safety regulations. Only use spare boards from sealed, undamaged packaging, because that is the only way to be sure they have no ESD or other damage. When you return a defective assembly, keep it ESD-protected, for example by wrapping it in ESD foil or reusing the packaging the new part came in. The sources give no figures on how well these measures work. One trainer says a wrist strap tied to equipotential bonding minimizes damage, but the real goal is to avoid damage altogether.
