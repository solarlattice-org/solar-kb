---
id: comp:heat-sink
type: Component
name: Heat sink
---
The heat sink is the metal cooling body inside an inverter or battery inverter. It carries heat away from the power electronics. Its temperature is measured and shown in degrees Celsius. On Sunny Island units it is shown for the device, and in a cluster the master displays it. Readings are also given for each connected Sunny Island Charger. Larger central inverters can have several heat sink temperature points, each logged as its own measurement. The main failure tied to this part is heat sink overtemperature. On the master unit it is reported as a fault, and on slave units it is reported as a warning. When the heat sink or transformer gets too hot, the device reduces its output power (derating) and logs a warning. The heat sink power unit needs almost no maintenance. A visual check is usually enough, because protective grids catch most of the dirt. Cleaning is normally needed only after an inverter failure. If the heat sink is dirty, clean it with compressed air and never with a vacuum cleaner.
